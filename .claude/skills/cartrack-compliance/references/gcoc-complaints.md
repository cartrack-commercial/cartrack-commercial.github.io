# Complaints under the General Code of Conduct

**Verified 2 October 2026** against the GCOC (BN 80 of 2003, as amended June 2020) and the FSCA
RE5 qualifying-criteria matrix. Re-verify before citing in anything signed.

## The framework is ss16–19, not s17

The Telesure due diligence cited **"General Code of Conduct, Section 17"** and the first draft of
Cartrack's complaints documents followed it. That is too narrow:

- **GCOC s16** — definitions (below). This is the one that was missed.
- **GCOC s17** — internal complaint resolution; categorising, recording and reporting.
- **GCOC s18** — record-keeping and reporting of complaints.
- **GCOC s19** — further complaints provisions.
- **FAIS Act ss20 and 27** — the Ombud for FSPs.
- **Ombud Council Rules for the Ombud for Financial Services Providers, 2024.**

## Defined terms — use these, not general usage

**"reportable complaint"** means any complaint *other than* one that has been —
(a) upheld immediately by the person who initially received the complaint;
(b) upheld within the provider's ordinary processes for handling client queries for that type of
product or service, **provided that process took no more than five business days** from receipt; or
(c) submitted in such a manner that the provider had no reasonable opportunity to record the
prescribed details.

**"upheld"** — finalised wholly or partly in the complainant's favour, where the complainant has
explicitly accepted that the matter is fully resolved (or it is reasonable to assume so) and all
undertakings have been met.

**"rejected"** — not upheld; the provider regards the matter as finalised having advised the
complainant it will take no further action. Includes complaints the provider regards as unjustified
or invalid, and those where the complainant does not accept or respond to proposals to resolve.

**"compensation payment"** — a payment (money, benefit or service) to compensate for proven or
estimated financial loss **where the provider accepts liability**. *Excludes* goodwill payments,
amounts contractually due, and refunds of amounts not contractually due. Includes interest on late
payment of the latter two.

**"goodwill payment"** — a payment to resolve a complaint as an expression of goodwill, **where the
provider does not accept liability** for any financial loss.

**"internal complaint resolution system and procedures"** — the system the provider establishes and
maintains under the Code for resolving client complaints.

**"Ombud"** — the Ombud for Financial Services Providers.

## Why the distinction matters

The obligation is to **categorise, record and report on *reportable* complaints**. A complaint
resolved on the spot, or inside five business days through ordinary query handling, is a complaint
but not a reportable one. A return that reports a single undifferentiated "complaints" number does
not answer the Code, and a reviewer who knows the Code will notice.

## Declaring a nil position

A nil register proves nothing by itself. This is what produced six Major Concern findings: an empty
register was sent with no declaration. A nil return should:

1. **Derive** the reportable figure — complaints received, less those upheld immediately, less those
   upheld within five business days.
2. Declare **compensation payments** and **goodwill payments** separately. Both are defined terms
   and both are commonly forgotten.
3. Cover escalations, Ombud referrals (naming the Ombud for FSPs **and** any other scheme),
   determinations, and complaints outstanding at period end.
4. State that **no categorisation was required because no reportable complaint arose, and that the
   categorisation framework exists regardless**. Say both halves.
5. Explain, in writing, why no complaint *samples* accompany it — absence of records reflecting
   absence of complaints, not absence of record-keeping.
6. Be **signed by a Key Individual**. An unsigned nil return is the same gap in a different shape.

## Separation of duties — check who holds which role

A representative whose advice is complained about takes no part in deciding that complaint. Where
one person holds more than one role, the escalation route must still land somewhere independent.

At Cartrack, **Brendan Kruger is both the Complaints Officer and a Key Individual**, so
"escalate to the KI" is not an independent route for a complaint about the Complaints Officer. Those
pass to the **second Key Individual (Jennie Allen)** and the **Compliance Officer (Daniel Opperman)**.
Say this explicitly in the Responsibility Schedule rather than leaving a reviewer to find it.
