Part one · the question that decides the fleet

Is a car covered while it is on an Uber or Bolt trip?

Your cars carry fare-paying passengers every day, driven by people who rent them from you. That is what any fleet policy you hold has to answer for first.

Renasa today: built for e-hailing Your policy is Renasa's e-hailing product. Every one of the 136 items records an Uber or Bolt inspection certificate and registration with more than one operator. That is the right starting point. Two things still need confirming in writing: the passenger and third party limits, which are not printed anywhere on the schedule, and how the clause excluding cover "if the vehicle is rented or hired out to a third party and is no longer under the control of the insured or a driver employed by the insured" applies to drivers who rent from you.
⚠ AC&E as quoted: e-hailing is not mentioned The AC&E quotation describes your business as "Rent-to-own and also short term and long term vehicle rentals" and rates every car for "Private & Business" use. Its 32 pages never mention e-hailing, Uber, Bolt or fare-paying passengers, and its vehicle definition reads "minibuses (excluding taxis)". Undisclosed use can void a policy or a claim. Until AC&E confirms in writing that the cars are covered on e-hailing trips, and that its R2,500,000 passenger liability covers fare-paying passengers, its price is not comparable with your current policy.
⚠ A driver who does not bring the car back AC&E's absconding clause gives no theft cover where a car is "hired through false pretences", where "conversion occurs", "failure to return leased vehicle occurs" or "absconding with the vehicle occurs", and theft must involve forcible and violent entry. Renasa's hiring-out wording has a similar effect. On both policies, a renter who keeps a car is not an insured theft. Your protection there is the tracking unit and its recovery service.
Tracking: a condition of theft cover on both Renasa requires a trip-log tracking unit on every car over R80,000, or theft and accident cover fall away. AC&E requires an early-warning tracker with a recovery contract on 63 cars (all 57 Polos, the NP200 and five cars over R399,999) and a passive tracker on 8 more, or there is no theft or hijack cover on that car. Every car on your schedule shows "OwnTracker". We will confirm with AC&E whether it qualifies.

Our recommendation

Both quotations are worth pursuing. Neither is ready to sign.

The fleet: AC&E, once e-hailing is accepted in writing On the same 126 cars AC&E is R20,528 a month cheaper and includes an excess waiver. We recommend moving only once AC&E confirms in writing that the cars are covered on Uber and Bolt trips with passenger liability for fare-paying passengers, re-quotes the full current fleet, and confirms which trackers qualify. Before cancelling, we will also check the profit share Renasa owes you, so nothing accrued is forfeited.
The dealership: Western, once four things are restored Western saves R8,454 a month and lifts public liability to R2.5 million. We recommend it once it (1) covers test drives and demo use and passengers in stock vehicles, (2) keeps your retro dates and adds products liability so the umbrella can attach, (3) returns the Ranger MS70HNGP to comprehensive, and (4) names Kundiso Investments if needed.
