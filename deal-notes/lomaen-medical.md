## ⛔ THE FINDING — the current liability policy excludes what this business does

The CIB public liability section carries **endorsement "WORK AWAY EXCLUSION – 001"**:

> *"The Company will not indemnify the Insured in respect of liability consequent upon death, injury
> or damage caused by or through or in connection with work done away from the Insured premises."*

**Lomaen's business is "import of x-ray machines, marketing, installing and repair of new machines."
Every installation and every repair happens at a customer's site.** Their liability policy does not
respond to any of it. And there is **no products liability and no defective workmanship** on the
current schedule at all.

| | Current CIB / Guardrisk | Bryte |

| **Work away** | ⛔ **EXCLUDED by endorsement** | ✅ **INCLUDED in the R10m** |

**Closing both holes costs R221.68 a month, inside a programme that is R8,840 a month cheaper.**
That is the whole pack in one sentence, and it is what Bronwan should lead with.

The retroactive date question also resolves itself: the CIB schedule says **01/06/2017**, which is

## 🚨 THE GATE — the sprinkler waiver

The CIB schedule records, under the fire section conditions:

> **"SURVEY DONE — Stacking Heights exceeds 3m. sprinkler system waived."**

The Bryte quotation is **"subject to a satisfactory survey prior to inception"**.

**If Bryte's surveyor does not grant the same waiver on R73.3m of stock stacked above three metres,
the quotation changes or the client is asked to sprinkler the warehouse — a capital project, not a
premium adjustment.** This is the single biggest risk to the deal. **Put the existing survey and
waiver in front of Bryte's underwriter now and ask them to confirm the waiver in writing before
anything goes to the client.**

Also on the current fire section and to be matched: **all fire hose reels must be tested annually
for water pressure by an accredited SAQCC-registered technician**, failing which the insurer is not
liable. Confirm Bryte's equivalent condition.

## ⚠️ What still needs fixing before this goes to the client

### 1 · The monthly figures on both quotes are wrong

**Sasria's monthly column is divided by 10, not 12.** On the full quote Sasria annual R33,754.69
appears as R3,375.47 a month; the correct figure is **R2,812.89**. Every other section divides
correctly by 12.

- Full cover: quote says **R42,868.39**/mo, the true figure is **R42,305.81** — overstated R562.58
- Vehicles only: quote says **R15,051.34**/mo, the true figure is **R15,002.46** — overstated R48.88

Small in rand, but if the client checks the arithmetic and it does not hold, everything else in the
pack comes into question. **Ask Bryte to reissue with the monthly column corrected.**

### 2 · The liability retroactive date contradicts itself on the face of the quote

The Combined Liability block is headed **"RETROACTIVE DATE: INCEPTION DATE OF POLICY"** and then,
three lines down, states **"Retroactive date 01 June 2017 will be retained"**. Bronwan's note says
maintained, which is the right answer — but **the schedule must be corrected before inception**, in
writing. A claims-made policy with two retroactive dates printed on it is an argument waiting to
happen, and eight years of past work turns on which one governs.

### 3 · The liability section is rated on nothing

**"TURNOVER R0"** and **"WAGES R0"** are printed on the Combined Liability block. Liability is rated
on turnover and wages, so as issued the R5,576.00 is a placeholder that **will be re-rated after
inception**. Get the real turnover and wage roll to Bryte now. *(Same shape as the Powerflow PI
turnover finding — a liability premium rated on a blank is not a premium.)*

### 4 · Stock at the customer's site is covered for four weeks, and not for accidental damage

Business all risks carries **"Stock subsequent to off-loading post conveyance at the consignee"
R300,000** at 6.000%. The conditions attached:

> *Cover limited to fire and allied perils in a secure covered building; theft subject to forcible
> and violent entry or exit from a locked building; **excluding all accidental loss or damage** and
> consequential losses.* · *Cover is in force for a **maximum 4 weeks** post delivery and still
> under the insured's custody and control pre-handover.*

For a company whose business is **installing** x-ray machines, the window between delivery and
handover is exactly when the machine is most exposed — and an installation and commissioning often
runs longer than four weeks. **Ask for the period extended and accidental damage included**, and
confirm the R300,000 limit is enough for one machine.

### 5 · There is no business interruption cover — and there is none today either

No BI section is quoted by Bryte, and the CIB schedule records **Business Interruption R0.00 —
"Cover in force: No"**. So this is a **standing gap, not a regression**. A business with **R73.3m of
stock**, a single risk address and import lead times cannot trade through a fire without it.
Neither broker has ever put it on the table. **Quote it.**

### 6 · Sasria looks under-declared on the motor section

The Sasria block records **3 private vehicles** and **3 commercial under 3,500kg**, and
**R0 against "Motor — commercial 3,500kg and over"** — on a fleet of **11 items that includes two
Hino trucks** (the 300 814 LWB at R783,833 is almost certainly over 3,500kg GVM). Five items appear
to carry no Sasria at all. Query the categories and the vehicle count.

### 7 · The insurer's name

Every page footer reads **"Quotation – Hollard Insurance Company"** on a **Bryte**-titled quotation.
**This is the same footer discrepancy as the Hamisa Group quotes.** Confirm in writing which company
carries the risk before anything is signed.

### 8 · The standing gates

Printed on both quotes: **subject to a satisfactory survey prior to inception**, **subject to full
three years' claims history**, and *"Underwriting needs claims experience to raise new business"*.
Also *"Prepared by: TBA"* and *"Claims: R30,000"* with no context — confirm what that R30,000 is.
Quotes valid **30 days** from issue, so the full-cover quote runs to **19 September 2026**.

### 9 · Alarm warranty

Both the **Theft** and **Electronic Equipment** sections carry **"ALARM WARRANTY TO APPLY TO RISK
PREMISES"**. Confirm the alarm specification, that it is serviced, and that it is armed out of
hours — a warranty breach voids those sections, not just the claim.
