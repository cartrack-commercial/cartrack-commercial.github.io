## 🚨 FINDING 1: the nil-excess theft benefit is written to a COMPETITOR'S BRAND

From the HCV and trailer excess table, verbatim:

> "**Ctrack** Tracking and Recovery Device operational on Heavy Commercial Vehicle or Trailer when
> the entire combination is stolen or hijacked in the same event: **NILL Excess**"

against, one line above:

> "Tracking or Recovery Device operational: **10% of the value of the vehicle**"

**Ctrack is a competitor of Cartrack, and Cartrack Insurance Agency is the broker on this very
quote.** As written, a client running Cartrack units pays 10% of the vehicle value on a hijack where
a Ctrack client pays nothing. **On the dearest truck (R2,012,670) that is R201,267 a claim. On the
dearest trailer (R2,689,098) it is R268,910.**

⛔ **Get Natsure to add Cartrack to that clause in writing before this quote goes anywhere.** It is
a one-line endorsement and it is worth more than most of the premium difference. Until it is fixed
the client's own policy wording steers them to a competitor's hardware.

(Note the same clause appears twice, once under HCV/Busses and once under Trailers, so it needs
fixing in both places.)

## 🚨 FINDING 2: the two buses have NO Sasria

The Sasria ME section covers three items only:

| Sasria item | Sum insured |
|---|---|
| Goods vehicles M2 | R2,917,060 (the 6 LDVs) |
| Goods vehicles exceeding 3,500kg M8, trucks | R53,132,476 |

Fleet sum insured is **R61,174,651**. The difference is **R1,738,186, which is exactly the two
buses**. Buses are a Sasria category in their own right and they have not been declared. Riot and
public disorder is not a remote risk for a vehicle moving healthcare waste. **Get them added.**

## 🚨 FINDING 3: clean-up is R250,000 a truck on a hazardous waste fleet

Optional extensions: **"Clean up Costs, Yes, R11,250,000, R2,250.00/mo"** on **45 HCV**, at an
annual premium per unit of R600. R11,250,000 ÷ 45 = **R250,000 per vehicle**.

On a fleet carrying **medical and hazardous waste**, R250,000 is the cost of a contained spill on a
yard, not an incident on a public road or near a water course. **Ask Natsure to price R1,000,000,
R2,500,000 and R5,000,000 per vehicle, and to confirm in writing whether R11,250,000 is an annual
aggregate across the fleet or a per-vehicle limit.** At R600 a year per truck the cover is priced
like an afterthought, which suggests the limit can be lifted cheaply.

⚠️ **And it is on the 45 trucks only.** The 2 trailers, 6 LDVs and 2 buses carry no clean-up line at
all. Same shape as Bakers Tankers, where the product travels in the trailer.

⚠️ **Nothing in this quote is environmental impairment liability.** Clean-up costs and third party
liability are not the same thing as statutory environmental liability under NEMA. On a hazardous
waste operator that gap needs naming, and it is the single best reason to look at a dedicated
environmental product alongside the motor (the Envirosure Spill Protect route used on Bakers).

## 🚨 FINDING 4: third party liability is an AGGREGATE of R5,000,000, not layers

HCV/TRL Sub-Section B, verbatim:

| | Limit |
|---|---|
| (a) Passenger liability (extension 2 and/or 3) | R2,500,000 |
| (b) Any occurrence due to fire and explosion | R5,000,000 |
| (c) **Any other event and the aggregate of (a), (b) and (c)** | **R5,000,000** |

So passenger liability does **not** sit on top of the third party limit, it sits inside it. One
serious incident involving both injury and property damage is capped at R5,000,000 in total.
**Compare this line directly against the current policy before anything is signed**, because it is
the kind of wording that reads like R7.5m of cover and is R5m.

LDV and private: third party R2,500,000, contingent liability R1,000,000, passenger and unauthorised
passenger R2,500,000 each.

negotiated. Same finding as Cordiguard Security, where a night loading was applied to a fleet that
works at night.
