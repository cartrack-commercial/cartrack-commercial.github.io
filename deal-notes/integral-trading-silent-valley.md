## The decisive regression — third-party liability

Santam rates third-party liability **by vehicle class**. Petrosure applies **R2,500,000 flat to all 27**.

| Class | Vehicles | Santam | Petrosure | Cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Truck-tractors (the horses) | 10 | **R10,000,000** | R2,500,000 | **−75%** |
| Tankers & heavy trailers | 13 | **R5,000,000** | R2,500,000 | −50% |
| Light vehicles | 4 | R2,500,000 | R2,500,000 | same ✓ |

A diesel horse pulling a loaded 4-compartment tanker drops from R10m to R2.5m. One highway
accident with injuries or a fire and R2.5m is nowhere near enough. **This is the same finding as
Twin Trans** — cheapest quote, decisive cover halved on a fuel tanker.

## Second regression — the goods-in-transit excess

Both carry **R1,000,000 per load**. The excess is not comparable.

| On a R1m load | Santam | Petrosure |
|---|---|---|
| Own damage | **R20,000** | 10% = **R100,000** |
| Theft / hijack | **R20,000** | 10% + 20% = **R300,000** |

Santam's base is 10% own damage / 25% theft, **bought down by excess reducers to a flat R20,000**
(reducer insured amounts R100,000 and R250,000). Petrosure has no reducer. Diesel-load hijacking is
the classic SA exposure on this risk — that is R300,000 out of pocket instead of R20,000.

Also on Santam's cargo section and **absent from the Petrosure quote**: territorial limits covering
**twelve countries** (RSA, Namibia, Botswana, Lesotho, Eswatini, Zimbabwe, Malawi, Mozambique,
Zambia, Tanzania, Angola, DRC), salvage/recovery of load R25,000, claims prep R25,000, fire
extinguishing R10,000. Petrosure states no territory at all and leaves **"Annual Carry Figure"
blank** — a GIT section rated without a carry figure will be re-rated.

## Motor excesses — better on small claims, worse where it counts

| | Santam (heavy) | Petrosure |
|---|---|---|
| Own damage | 10%, **min R30,000** | 10%, **min R5,000** ✓ better |
| Theft / hijack | 15%, min R7,500 | 10% + 10% = **20%** ✗ |
| Single-vehicle accident | *no loading* | **additional 10%** ✗ |
| Night driving | 5% (23h00–04h00) | 10% (22h00–04h00) ✗ |
| Light vehicles | 5%, min R4,000 | 10%, min R5,000 ✗ |

**The single-vehicle loading matters most.** A tanker rollover is the classic single-vehicle loss:
on the R2.4m trailer that is 20% = R480,000 at Petrosure against 10% = R240,000 at Santam.

## 🚨 Neither policy covers pollution

Santam: **"Environmental impairment liability — Not included"**, on every vehicle. Petrosure has no
such section anywhere. For a business whose entire operation is *transportation of diesel*, a
rollover contaminating soil or a water course is the catastrophic exposure, and **neither policy
answers it.** Place **EIL separately** — same conclusion as Waste Carriers. This is a current gap,
not a regression, but it should be the first thing Cules raises.

## Other gates before anything goes to the client

1. **Retroactive date is not stated on the Petrosure liability section.** Santam is **claims-made
   with retro 20 August 2021** — five years of history. If Petrosure resets to inception that is
   five years of past work uninsured. Must be pinned to 20/08/2021 or earlier.
2. **Santam's public liability is unlimited in the period**; Petrosure states R10m per event with no
   aggregate shown. Confirm.
3. **Basis of settlement is not stated on the Petrosure quote.** Santam carries **Agreed Value on 23
   of 27** (10% variance) and retail on the four light vehicles. Retail book value is meaningless for
   a custom GRW or Trailer & Tanker Technology tanker — agreed value must be confirmed in writing.
4. **MG45VLGP is under-insured by R300,000** on the Petrosure quote: R1,516,105 against Santam's
   R1,816,105. Fleet totals **R31,311,815** (Santam) vs **R31,011,815** (Petrosure). Same 27 vehicles
   otherwise, matched by registration.
5. **The quote is not firm.** "Loss History: **Tba**", "Security details: **Tba**", and the footer
   reads *subject to satisfactory claims history · rates subject to change · risk may be subject to
   survey · additional premium may be levied for facultative reinsurance*. Petrosure has quoted
   blind on a R31m fleet.
6. **Sasria is R1,974.38 higher** at Petrosure. Sasria is a statutory pass-through at identical
   rates everywhere — a difference means a different exposure was declared. Query it.
7. **Broker fee line is blank** on the Petrosure quote. Settle Cartrack's remuneration before it goes
   anywhere near the client.
8. **Roadside assistance disappears.** Santam includes 24-hour motor emergency assistance (R13.00);
   Petrosure lists roadside "only available on request".
9. **Santam excludes petroleum products (retail)** from its products liability. Worth understanding
   what that means for this operation before assuming the Petrosure products cover is equivalent.

## The Petrosure quote is a forecourt template

It carries chilled refrigerated stock, petrol pumps & forecourt property, subsidence in respect of
fuel in **underground tanks**, till limits, petrol attendants, car-wash valet, forecourt negligence,
motor traders internal & external, "premises to have a drop safe". **This client has no forecourt.**
Meanwhile what a haulier actually needs — pollution liability, stated cross-border GIT terms,
agreed-value confirmation — is missing. Same pattern as the **Hamisa / Kgahlisa Petrosure quote**.

## Cartrack angle

Santam's schedule records **Cartrack on 16 of 27 vehicles** (13 Tag Range with recovery, 2 Telematics
Range, 1 Fleet Management), plus Tracker Network on one and Capital Air on the Mercedes. **Roughly
eight vehicles have no tracking device recorded.** Petrosure requires **tracking on every vehicle
over R250,000** — about 23 of the 27 — plus pre-inspection. So the units are a **binding
precondition**, not an upsell, and Cartrack is already the incumbent on most of the fleet.
