01
Worth more to you than the premium

Your surge protection does not meet your own policy

This one has nothing to do with changing insurer. It is on your current commercial schedule, it is live today, and it can be fixed in a morning.

What the policy requires
Fire section, special conditions "A surge protection device must be installed in the main electrical distribution board of your premises by a qualified electrician. The device must be South African National Standards (SANS) approved with a maximum discharge current (Imax) of at least 40kA. If damage is caused by lightning or power surge and the required surge protection was not installed or in working condition, then the insured will be responsible for the first 20% of the value of the claim."
What your schedule declares
Summary of insured premises · surge protection
"Db protection (Imax less than 40kA)"
What that means in money Plant, machinery and all other contents are insured for R10,000,000, and this is a refrigeration business. A 20% penalty on a serious surge or lightning loss runs to seven figures — and your schedule already records a lightning claim on 29 December 2023.
What we suggest Have a qualified electrician fit a SANS-approved surge protection device rated at 40kA or above in the main distribution board, and send us the certificate. Do this whether or not you move a single policy. It is the cheapest risk improvement in this document by a wide margin.
Two related conditions worth checking at the same time Your policy requires a valid certificate of compliance for all solar system installations (you carry R120,000 of solar), and it makes theft, fire and malicious damage cover conditional on an approved alarm being radio-linked to 24-hour monitoring and armed reaction and armed whenever the premises is unoccupied. Every security device on your schedule is recorded as "Unspecified" — we would like to have those confirmed and properly noted.

Three gaps on the commercial policy

What is not covered today

We are recommending you keep this policy. That is not the same as saying it is right. These three are worth fixing at OUTsurance now, and they carry over to whoever writes it in 2028.

1 · Business interruption is R3,000,000 against an R89,000,000 turnover Gross profit is insured at R3,000,000 with a twelve-month indemnity period. On a wholesale butchery turning over R89 million, that figure looks far too low — even on a thin margin the gross profit would be several times it. A fire that stops this business for a year is insured for a fraction of what it would actually cost you. Please let us have the real gross profit off your financials so the section can be re-rated. The alternative quotation carries exactly the same R3,000,000, so this is not a criticism of OUTsurance — it is a figure nobody has revisited.
2 · Nothing covers the stock spoiling Deterioration of stock is shown as an optional extension on the machinery breakdown section and has not been taken. Deterioration of goods in transit is optional on the goods-in-transit section and has not been taken either. You carry R2.68 million of food processing equipment including five heat-exchanger blowers and freezers and fifteen heat exchangers. For a meat business, refrigeration failing and the stock spoiling is the characteristic loss — and it is the one loss neither policy answers. We would like to quote it on both sections.
3 · Goods in transit — the description and the driver The section insures R700,000 per load against a declared annual carry of R282,000,000 and 91 to 120 transits a month. Two things concern us:
The type of goods is declared as "agricultural produce." You move chilled and frozen meat. A commodity description that does not match what is actually on the truck is the first thing an assessor looks at.
Driver fidelity has not been taken. Your schedule states it plainly: "There is no cover for criminal involvement of the insured's driver or employees in theft, hijacking, robbery or attempt thereat."
We would also like to reconcile the R282 million annual carry against your R89 million turnover — the section is rated on that figure.
