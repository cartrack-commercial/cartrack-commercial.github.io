The gap that has been open for two years

No public liability. Not on any of the three.

Straight off the index of cover Combined Liability (claims-made): NO. Umbrella Liability: NO. Employer's Liability: NO. Only 7 of 24 sections are taken. The only liability cover anywhere in the programme is the motor third party limit of R2,500,000.

On a business that puts sharps containers into hospitals and clinics, collects and transports biohazardous waste, and runs hazardous waste training on other people's premises. Since at least 1 August 2024.

Why this is the sale

It is not a criticism of Natsure or Alpha, and it is not something the incumbent can fix with a discount. It is the single most serious thing in the file and we are the ones who found it. A broker who reads 175 pages and finds the hole is worth changing broker for. That is the whole pitch.

Also missing on all three
  • Business interruption
  • Goods in transit
  • Fidelity, money, glass
  • Machinery breakdown on R12.8m of plant
  • Deterioration of stock
  • Fare-paying passenger liability on both midibuses
What I have asked both markets for Public liability, products liability and employers' liability, priced as an addition, plus a separate spill solution. Expect the programme to come back dearer than R423,308 and be ready to defend that. A cheaper policy that still has no public liability is not a win, and if it goes wrong it is our Record of Advice that gets read.
Careful with the clean-up line Natsure's R250,000 a vehicle is a motor extension, not environmental impairment liability under NEMA. It is on the 45 trucks only, nothing on the two trailers, and the trailer is where the product sits. Same route as Bakers: get Envirosure to price a transport spill plan so the client can see the real number.
Warranties that void cover today
  • Firefighting equipment serviced annually, records kept
  • Gas installations to SANS 100087-1
  • Hot work permit, cover suspended from the moment of breach
  • Computer equipment excluded from fire
  • Micro-organism exclusion, absolute
The excess crossover, memorise this

Natsure is 4.5% of the vehicle's value min R5,000, so about R53,132 on the average truck whatever the claim. Alpha is 10% of the claim min R5,000. They cross at about R450,000. Below that Alpha is far better, above it Natsure is. Ask what their typical claim looks like.

Play 06
What you do now

Your moves, in order

  • 1
    Get the claims record from NuWest
    The schedule carries no loss history, only "no claim bonus: 0" per vehicle. Both quotes are conditional on three years' experience in writing, so neither price is firm. Do not present a final number until this lands.
  • 2
    Tracking survey on all 70
    Confirm what is fitted against the 50 that read "Required". Three competitor units are on the book. This is the 70-unit lead and it is also the service finding.
  • 3
    Both markets to price liability
    Public, products and employers'. Plus car hire, roadside and credit shortfall so the comparison is like for like.
  • 4
    Alpha to re-rate to R79,394,630
    And correct the X5 and M135i model years against the schedule.
  • 5
    Ask the client what shifts they run
    Natsure loads the excess 5% for driving between 23h00 and 04h00 and another 5% for a single-vehicle accident, cumulative. Alpha has no loadings. Healthcare waste often moves before dawn. This one question can decide the recommendation.
  • 6
    Align the inception dates
    Natsure's fleet quote incepts 1 October and its commercial quote 1 November. A month apart is a month of half-moved cover.
Bottom line for you Alpha is the better price, Natsure is the better policy, and neither is ready to bind. Alpha is R38,547 a month cheaper but is short R4.95m on values, gives no clean-up and matches rather than improves the third party limit. Natsure copies the book correctly, doubles third party and adds clean-up, but is only R28,878 better like for like. Once liability is priced the gap will narrow and my expectation is that Natsure wins on cover. The tracking point goes to the client this week regardless of who writes the risk.
