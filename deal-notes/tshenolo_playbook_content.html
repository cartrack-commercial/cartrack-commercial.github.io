⚠ The trailer is the point, not the truck Alpha's schedule is the first document that tells us what the trailers are: a 7.2m single skeletal drawbar trailer R697,831 and a G.R.P 15,500mm tridem axle trailer R2,689,098, registration "Required". Glass reinforced plastic on a tridem axle is a liquid body. Same lesson as Bakers Tankers: on a fleet like this the substance is in the trailer, and the clean-up line is written against the truck.
The same subject comes back at the premises, and it is a warranty not a suggestion Natsure's commercial policy conditions cover on storage of hazardous and flammable substances per OHS Act Regulation 4 and SANS 02363, waste bagged daily and removed weekly, a SAIDSA alarm linked to armed response with the arming log available, an electrical compliance certificate, fire equipment serviced annually, SABS Class I lightning arrestor and Class II surge arrestor, and stock raised 150mm on approved pallets.

Walk these with the client before inception. On a hazardous waste site a breached warranty is the cleanest route an insurer has to decline, and the client will assume nobody reads them. Same shape as the CS Continental surge-protection find, where the schedule declared a breach of its own condition.
How to play it Do not accuse anyone. Say: "Alpha lists dangerous goods liability as an option on your own schedule and it is switched off. What does it cost?" At R600 a year per truck for R250,000 at Natsure, the answer will be small. If Alpha prices it and stays under Natsure, Alpha wins the account. If Alpha refuses, we recommend Natsure even though it is R38,547 dearer. Get it in writing from Alpha.
