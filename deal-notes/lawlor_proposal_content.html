⚠ What that means on a claim Santam is R12,500,000 short. Insurance policies carry an average clause: if the sum insured is less than the value at risk, the insurer pays the same proportion of the loss.
Your main house is insured at about two thirds of its value on that quotation. A total loss would settle at roughly 66 cents in the rand — you would carry around R10,360,000 of it yourself. And average applies to partial losses too, so a R2,000,000 fire pays about R1,320,000.
⚠ And the two collector cars are on the wrong basis Both the Porsche 911 GT3 RS and the 1968 Dodge Charger are quoted on retail value rather than agreed value.
Worse, the Dodge is described on the schedule as a "1996 Base Coupe". It is a 1968 Charger. A 1968 Charger has no meaningful retail book value — which is exactly why it is insured on an agreed value of R2,000,000 today. On the basis quoted, that figure is not what would be paid.
To be fair to Santam We do not think this is sharp practice — it looks like the quotation was built from incomplete information. It can be fixed, and if the excesses on page 04 matter to you, it is worth fixing rather than discarding. But it must not be accepted as it stands.

Read that again, because it is the largest single number in this review If the Porsche is damaged today, the first R200,000 is yours. On the Santam quotation it would be R5,500. Santam also waives the theft excess entirely where an approved tracking device is fitted.

That is a R194,500 difference on a single claim — more than seven years of the entire saving we are discussing. It is a genuine advantage and we are not going to talk you out of it.
So what do we actually advise? If the sums insured matter most to you: take Hollard. Cheapest, correct buildings, agreed value on both collectors, R30m liability.

If the excess matters most to you: go back to Santam and have the quotation rebuilt — at R38,880,000 of buildings, with agreed value on the Porsche and the Dodge, and the Dodge correctly described. Then compare it again properly. What we will not do is let you take Santam as it is quoted today, because the excess saving is real and the buildings shortfall is much larger.

03
Before anything is signed

Three corrections on the Hollard quotation

We are recommending Hollard. That does not mean the quotation is right as issued — these three things must be fixed and confirmed in writing first.

  • 1
    The Porsche is R1,000,000 under-insured on the quote
    Your 2019 911 GT3 RS is insured today at R5,000,000 — R4,000,000 for the car plus the R1,000,000 Weisig Package. The Hollard quotation shows R4,000,000. The package has to be added back. This is the single most important correction in the file, and it is worth more than the annual saving.
  • 2
    The Dodge's basic excess reads "Unspecified"
    A blank excess is not a low excess — it is an unknown one. It must be stated as a rand figure on the schedule before inception.
  • 3
    Early-warning trackers on every vehicle, within 14 days
    Hollard makes this a condition of cover, not a suggestion. Your BMW M5 already carries a Cartrack CT1 and the 2023 D350 has a Tracker unit — so three vehicles need attention. If the fourteen days pass without the devices fitted and active, the theft and hijack cover falls away. We will arrange this so it is done inside the window.
Your claims history, for the record Two claims, both in 2021 — a geyser at R10,400 and lightning at R107,693. Nothing since. That is a clean record on a household of this size and it is part of why the market has come back competitively.
A disclosure we are obliged to make Replacing your CIB policy means any reduction in cover must be disclosed to you in writing before you decide. Everything we have found is set out in this document — the buildings shortfall and settlement basis at Santam, the Porsche sum insured at Hollard, the valuables gap on all three, and the liability change on the properties policy overleaf. ⚠ Two reductions hidden in one line Public liability moves from R50,000,000 on an occurrence basis to R20,000,000 on a claims-made basis.

The limit is cut by 60%. And the basis changes: an occurrence policy answers for anything that happened while it was in force, whenever the claim arrives. A claims-made policy only answers if the claim is made while the policy is live and the retroactive date reaches back far enough.

For R198.67 a month, we do not think that is a trade worth making. We have asked Tranquille to price R50,000,000 and to state the retroactive date in writing.
