Round four is the strongest position yet — and here is every way it still goes wrong.

1
The client may read "R2m is our max" as an answer.

Open since round 2. Santam gives R2.5m today. If we recommend OM as the safe option, this gap goes with it.
SAYChase OM before you present. Recommending the fallback with a known hole in it is not a fallback.

Stripped in June at the client's instruction. Riot and public-disorder damage to 34 buses is uninsured, on all three options.
SAYGet it in writing this round. Verbal instruction on a cover this size is not a defensible file.

If VAPS says it is intentional, the R32,048.87 figure evaporates and you have talked up a saving that isn't there.
SAYPresent it as a question you have raised, never as a number the client can bank. Confirm before you quote it.

The objections you will hit
"They said R2m is their maximum. So that's settled?"
That settles the amount. It doesn't settle whether the section pays at all. The wording covers e-hailing passengers and excludes non-e-hailing use — and you don't run e-hailing. That's what I've gone back on.
"R2m instead of R2.5m — is that really a problem?"
Half a million less on a full bus is a problem, yes. But it's the smaller one. The bigger one is a form written for Uber drivers sitting on a scholar transport fleet.
"We've been at this since June. Can we just decide?"
We can. Today, if you want: Old Mutual, R230 a month cheaper than now, passenger cover on clean wording at R2.5m per vehicle. Or give me one week for King Price's answer in writing and you may save R3,070 instead.
"Santam's motor is R10,000 more — why not just move?"
It isn't, quite. Santam prices passenger liability inside the motor rate; King Price charges it separately. Like-for-like the gap is nearer R5,700 — still a win, just not the one the raw numbers show.
