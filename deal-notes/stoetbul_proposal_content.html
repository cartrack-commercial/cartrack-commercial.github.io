And the excess comes down with it On the Everest you currently pay a R15,000 excess on every claim, and R7,610 on the Ranger. At Western the basic excess is 5% of the claim, minimum R2,500. On a R60,000 repair to the Everest that is R3,000 instead of R15,000.
One condition you must meet Western requires an approved tracking device on every vehicle worth more than R350,000 — that is your Jeep, the Everest and the Ranger. Your two Fords already have units (Tracetec and Matrix) which we will declare. The Jeep has neither a tracker nor an alarm on your current schedule and will need one fitted. Without it, a theft or hijack claim carries an extra excess of 20% of the vehicle's value.
Housekeeping The quotation shows no registration, VIN or engine numbers on any of the four vehicles. We will have all of them recorded before cover starts. We have also asked why car hire has been quoted on the Hummer and Jeep but not on the two Fords.

Why the retroactive date matters Liability here is written on a claims-made basis: it answers claims made during the policy year, but only for events after the retroactive date. When you change insurer that date usually resets to the day you move — which quietly drops everything before it. Western has quoted 13 June 2024, exactly as you have now. Nothing you have already served falls out of cover.
The one reduction, and what we are doing about it Products liability drops from R4,500,000 to R2,500,000. We have asked Western to match R4.5m. Note that food and drink liability sits alongside it at its own R2,500,000 limit, and is named on the schedule rather than left to argument.
Worth checking on your current policy Your Santam schedule describes the business simply as "Restaurant". The Western quotation describes it as "restaurant, pub and live music venue". If the venue trades as a pub with live music, the fuller description is the correct one — and it is worth making sure your insurer has always known that.
