## 🚨 THE FINDING — neither quote covers the core exposure

Gear Lab's business is **taking customers' vehicles onto a hoist and working on the gearbox.**
Every rand of exposure sits in the motor traders section, and both quotes have it switched off —
in opposite ways.

| | Hollard | ONE |
|---|---|---|
| **Vehicle hoists and ramps** | ⛔ **No** — on all four items | ✅ **Car hoists: Yes** |
| **Damage to vehicles being worked upon** | ⛔ excluded by the wording | ⛔ **No** |
| Contingent liability | ⛔ No | ✅ Yes |
| Material damage — customers' vehicles | R50,000 (internal) + R50,000 | **R50,000** |
| Third-party liability | R2,500,000 × 2 | R2,500,000 |
| Theft of vehicle parts and accessories | — | R10,000 |
| Loss of keys | — | R20,000 |
| Excess | to confirm | 10% of claim min R3,000; windscreen 25% min R1,000 |

**Hollard.** Specific exception 14 to Sub-Section A: *"The company shall not be liable under this
section in respect of loss, damage and/or injury occurring by or through the use of any vehicle
hoist or vehicle ramp **unless otherwise stated in the Schedule**."* It is **not** stated —
"Vehicle Hoists and Ramps: **No**" on every one of the four items. **A gearbox workshop puts every
vehicle it touches on a hoist.** Exception 11 also excludes *"defective workmanship or consequential
loss as a result thereof to any vehicle in or on the premises"*, and exception 5 excludes
*"mechanical, electronic or electrical breakdown, failure or breakage."*

**ONE.** Car hoists **are** included — good — but the internal risk records **"Damage to vehicles
being worked upon: No."** That is the sentence that describes what they do for a living.

**Neither quote, as issued, pays if a customer's car is dropped off a hoist or damaged on the
bench.** Both need those extensions quoted before either goes to the client.

**And R50,000 of material damage is too low regardless.** A modern SUV in for a gearbox rebuild is
worth R500,000 to R1,500,000, and a workshop of this type holds several customer vehicles at once.
Establish the maximum value on the premises at any one time and insure to it.

⚠️ **ONE's schedule contradicts itself on work away** — the internal risk extension list shows
**"WorkAway: No"** and **"WorkAway: Yes"** on consecutive lines. Get it resolved in writing.

**Two different registration numbers at the same address**, one a company registered in **2000** and
one registered in **2025**. **Insuring the wrong entity means no cover at claims stage.** Establish
which company trades, owns the premises, and holds the customers' vehicles, and put that name on

**The VAT number on the Hollard quote is invalid** — `9874563` is seven digits; a South African VAT

blank, not populated with a wrong number.

prior insurance — and it makes this a **new venture**, which is a different underwriting
proposition again. Tell the insurers rather than let them find out.

## What is not covered at all — the first-programme gaps

Neither quote carries any of the following. On a first programme that is normal; leaving it
unsaid is not.

- **Business interruption.** A workshop that burns has no income and still has salaries and rent.
  With stock at R5,200,000 and a single premises, this is the most serious omission.
- **Machinery breakdown** on the workshop equipment — hoists, presses, test benches.
- **Electronic equipment** — diagnostic equipment on a modern gearbox job is not optional.
- **Money**, **glass**, **fidelity** (theft by an employee), **goods in transit**.
- **Accidental damage.**
- **Employer's liability** — Hollard's combined liability is two R2,000,000 items; confirm whether
  either is EL, and confirm the split.
- **The client's own vehicles.** Neither quote has a motor section for vehicles Gear Lab owns.
  Establish whether they have any — a recovery bakkie is common in this trade.
- **Sasria on the motor traders section.** Hollard charges **M4 Motor Ferries & Traders R44.27**;
  **ONE charges no M4 at all.** Query it — Sasria is statutory and should not simply be absent.
