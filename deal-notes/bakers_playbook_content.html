The contradiction is now in OUTsurance's own words, twice The same schedules price environmental impairment liability R5,000,000 per vehicle on 52 trucks, and the conditions attaching to it read:
"(5) owners, drivers and operators must be familiar with legislation and other requirements relating to the transport of dangerous substances and goods. … a professional driving permit for vehicles transporting dangerous goods will also be required."
One page excludes the load. Another prices liability for it and tells the client which permits the drivers must carry. Put both paragraphs in front of Virgil Niekerk and ask which one governs.

Same shape as Twin Trans, where the cheaper quote had dropped pollution on a fuel tanker.

ONE's motor schedule prints no hazmat exclusion (the wording pages are not in the PDF) and its marine quote accepts diesel, petrol, paraffin and jet fuel with SANS, licensing and driver warranties. That is the contrast to use with Virgil.
