## ⛔ THE FINDING — do not move the commercial policy

The commercial saving is **R61.77 a month, R741.24 a year.**

The OUTsurance commercial schedule states:

**Moving the commercial policy to save R741 a year forfeits R42,637.61.** The bonus is worth
**fifty-seven years** of the saving. There is no version of that trade that works.

⚠️ **This corrects the 12 August note.** That note recorded *"OUT's schedule states the client does
not qualify for a Business OUTbonus on 31 Jul 2028 — so nothing is forfeited by leaving."* **That is
true of the FLEET policy only** — its claims ratio is far too high to qualify. The **commercial**
policy has a clean ratio and a live, projected bonus. The cash-back objection is dead on the fleet
and very much alive on the commercial.

**Recommendation: move the fleet to Western, leave the commercial where it is.** That captures
R109,871 a year, keeps the R42,637.61, and leaves us a clean run at the commercial account at its
**29 July** anniversary once the bonus has been paid.

## 🚨 What the commercial schedule exposed — five things worth more than the premium

The commercial policy costs R19,600.21 a month and, section by section, it is not in good shape.

### 1 · The surge protection does not meet the policy's own condition

The schedule records the declared protection as **"Db protection (Imax less than 40kA)"**.

The fire section's special conditions require: *"A surge protection device must be installed in the
main electrical distribution board … SANS approved with a maximum discharge current (Imax) of **at
least 40kA**. If damage is caused by lightning or power surge and the required surge protection was
not installed … the insured will be responsible for the **first 20% of the value of the claim**."*

**The declared device is below the required specification, on the face of the schedule.** Plant and
machinery is insured for **R10,000,000** and the business runs on refrigeration. A 20% penalty
excess on a serious surge loss is a seven-figure number — and **they already had a lightning claim
on 29 December 2023.** This is the single most valuable thing in the file and it costs the client a
sparky's morning to fix.

### 2 · Business interruption is R3,000,000 against an R89,000,000 turnover

Gross profit **R3,000,000**, 12-month indemnity period. **Identical on the Western quote**, so it
is a standing gap, not a regression.

A wholesale butchery turning over R89m does not have a R3m gross profit. Even on a thin 15% margin
the figure would be north of R13m. **A fire that stops this business for twelve months is insured
for a fraction of what it would cost.** Get the actual gross profit off the financials and re-rate.

### 3 · Deterioration of stock is not covered — on either side

- **Machinery breakdown → "Deterioration of stock extension: Optional"** — not taken. There is
  **R2,679,500 of food processing equipment** on the schedule, including **five heat-exchanger
  blowers/freezers and fifteen heat exchangers**.
- **Goods in transit → "Deterioration of goods in transit: Optional"** — not taken.
- The Western quote shows **"MACHINERY BREAKDOWN DETERIORATION OF STOCK R0,00"** — also not taken.

**For a meat business the characteristic loss is the refrigeration failing and the stock spoiling,
and neither the current policy nor the proposal covers it.** This was the right instinct on
12 August; now it is documented on both sides.

### 4 · Goods in transit is R700,000 against R282,000,000 of annual carry

Sum insured **R700,000** per conveyance (plus debris removal R150,000), excess **R15,500**,
**91–120 transits a month**, **annual amount in transit R282,000,000**.

Three problems:
- **Type of goods is declared as "Agricultural produce"** on a butchery. Meat is chilled foodstuff.
  The Western quote describes it properly as *"all property usual to the insured's business"*. **A
  mis-declared commodity is a repudiation argument.**
- **Driver fidelity: optional, not taken.** The schedule spells out the consequence: *"There is no
  cover for criminal involvement of the insured's driver or employees in theft, hijacking, robbery
  or attempt thereat."*
- **Annual carry R282m against turnover R89m** — 3.2×. One of those two figures is wrong and it
  matters, because the section is rated on carry.

### 5 · Public liability is R5,300,000, claims-made, retro **06 January 2026**

Seven months of retroactive protection on a food business. The condition attached says the retro
date only holds if there was **uninterrupted claims-made** public liability cover beforehand — and
they came from **Old Mutual Insure after 10+ years**. If OMI wrote it on an occurrence basis, or
with an earlier retro, **that history is gone.**

Defective products is included at R5,300,000 — good — but the section excludes *"defective design,
formula, plan or specification, where you are responsible for any part of the manufacturing,
packaging or preparation prior to sale."* **A butchery prepares and packages meat.** And products
liability is **RSA only**.

**R5.3m of public liability for a wholesale meat supplier with 40 staff and R89m of turnover is
light.** One listeriosis-style incident exceeds it.
