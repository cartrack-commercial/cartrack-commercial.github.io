Why the trailers matter more than the trucks Alpha's schedule is the first document to tell us what your two trailers actually are: a 7.2m single skeletal drawbar trailer at R697,831, and a G.R.P 15,500mm tridem axle trailer at R2,689,098. Glass reinforced plastic on a tridem axle is a liquid body. That is the single unit on this fleet most likely to spill, and neither quotation carries one rand of clean-up cover against it.
Why R250,000 a vehicle is the wrong order of magnitude A spill of clinical or hazardous waste on a public road is not a cleaning bill. It brings in the municipality and the provincial department, it closes a road, and the remediation is charged to the operator. Add third party property damage and the cost of the load itself and R250,000 is reached before the first invoice from the contractor.

That is the limit on the dearer of the two quotations. The cheaper one carries nothing at all. Neither insurer has been asked for a realistic figure, which is the actual finding here.
⚠ And the same subject comes back as a warranty on the premises The Natsure commercial policy makes cover at your sites conditional on, among others, storage of hazardous and flammable substances in accordance with the OHS Act Regulation 4 and SANS 02363, and on waste being bagged daily and removed from the buildings at least once a week. There is also a SABS Class I lightning arrestor and Class II surge arrestor condition and a SAIDSA alarm linked to armed response with the arming log available to the insurer.

On a hazardous waste site these are not paperwork. They are the conditions on which a claim is paid or declined, and they should be walked through before inception rather than after a loss.
✅ What we have asked for Alpha to price dangerous goods liability, spillage and clean-up costs on the trucks and the trailers, and tool of trade liability. Natsure to quote a clean-up limit above R250,000 a vehicle and to extend it beyond the trucks. At R2,250 a month for R11,250,000, this is not expensive cover. It is simply cover nobody has asked for.

The gap that is on every document

Neither quotation contains public liability

We have three quotation documents in front of us, between R423,308 and R461,855 a month. Not one of them carries a public liability section, a products liability section or an employers' liability section. The only liability anywhere on either programme is motor third party, which responds to the use of a vehicle and to nothing else.

⚠ What that leaves uncovered You supply speci-bins, biohazardous tape and cable ties into hospitals and clinics. You handle biohazardous material on other people's premises. You run hazardous waste management training.

A container that fails in a ward, a needlestick injury during a collection, contaminated waste that enters the wrong stream, or advice given in a training session: none of those is a motor claim, and none of them is covered by anything on these documents.
Which of two things this is, and only you can tell us Either you hold a separate liability policy that we have not been shown, in which case the proposals as quoted would leave it behind and it must be replaced or kept alongside.

Or you do not hold one, in which case this is the most important thing on any page of this pack, and it is worth more than the R38,547 a month that separates the two quotations.

Your current schedule answers it. Nothing else will.

Where this lands

What we recommend, and what has to happen first

Our recommendation Alpha, on condition that it prices the three things it has left out. It is R38,547 a month cheaper, its excess structure is materially kinder on ordinary claims, and it is the only document that has given us a full vehicle schedule.

If Alpha will not price dangerous goods liability and clean-up, then Natsure, despite being dearer. On a hazardous waste fleet we will not recommend the cheaper programme when it is the one with no pollution cover at all.

Read the basic excess carefully, because the two work differently Natsure charges a percentage of the vehicle, so on an average truck that is roughly R45,000 whatever the claim, including a small one. Alpha charges a percentage of the claim.

The crossover is around R450,000. Below it Alpha is far better. Above it Natsure is. Most fleet claims are well below R450,000, which is why we read this as a point in Alpha's favour, but you should know it cuts both ways on a total loss.
