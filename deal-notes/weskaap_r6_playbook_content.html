The objections you'll hit, and the words that answer them
"Old Mutual was only R230 cheaper. Why is ONE R3,000 cheaper? What's the catch?"
Two things. ONE prices the fleet as one flat premium instead of 51 rates, and there is no R2,900 broker fee. The catch is real and small: the excess on a bus claim is 10% of the claim, and a new bus must be declared the day you buy it. Both are in writing in my advice.
"You said the same thing about King Price in July."
I said King Price was cheaper and that its passenger wording did not fit you, and I would not let you sign. That is the same test I am applying here: the R5 million must say fare-paying on the schedule before I put it in front of you as final.
"Why did the ONE quote come in at R36,000 and now you say R34,600?"
Because ONE insured R7.9 million of your buses as a building at the depot. It is a copying error from the Santam schedule, and I sent it back the day I saw it. The corrected figure is theirs to confirm, not mine to promise.
"What about Sasria on the buses?"
Neither policy has it, because you took it off in June. That was your call and I am asking you to confirm it in writing again, so nobody can later say it was mine.
"I don't want trackers on old buses."
ONE needs them on ten: anything over R500,000 plus the Hilux, Fortuner and Quantum. Without them those ten have no theft cover. The saving pays for them, and you get live location on the buses carrying your scholars, which your WCED contract will like.
