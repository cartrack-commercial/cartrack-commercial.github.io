## 3 · Goods in transit — the excess, one more time

This is the item that decides the deal, and it is directly connected to point 2.

The insured's losses are hijackings of a load. On a total loss of a R1,000,000 diesel load:

| | Current policy | W5101 as quoted |
|---|---|---|
| Own damage | **R20,000** | 10% = **R100,000** |
| Theft / hijack | **R20,000** | 10% + 20% = **R300,000** |

The current policy carries two first-amount-payable reducers (insured amounts R100,000 and
R250,000) which bring both down to a flat **R20,000**. I understand your excess-provider
arrangement is not finalised. Two questions:

1. **When do you expect it to be in place?** If it is weeks rather than months we would rather wait
   than move the client onto a R300,000 hijack excess.
2. **Failing a reducer, will the underwriter consider a flat first amount payable** on the goods
   section — R50,000 would still be materially worse than the R20,000 the client has today, but it
   is a number we could put in front of them. A percentage excess on a hijacked load is not.

**As it stands, one hijacking costs the insured R280,000 more than their current policy, against an
annual saving of R101,037.** On this client's record specifically, that is not a trade we can
recommend, and I would rather tell you that plainly than waste your time.
