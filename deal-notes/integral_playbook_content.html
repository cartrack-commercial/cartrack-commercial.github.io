The Naked Truth

DO NOT HAND
TO THE CLIENT

You are walking in to argue against a saving. That is an unusual position and a sharp client will test it. Here is where you are weak.

1
You are telling a business to spend R114,000 a year more.
That is the whole meeting in one sentence, and if you are not comfortable saying it out loud you will sound evasive. The honest framing: you are not asking them to spend more, you are asking them not to sell R7.5m of liability cover for R9,516 a month.
SAY"I'd rather bring you a saving I can defend than one I can't. Give me a week with the underwriter."
2
Petrosure is genuinely better in three places.
Small accident excess min R5,000 against Santam's R30,000. Statutory legal defence R250k against R50k. Wrongful arrest R100k against R50k. If you pretend the quote is all bad, the client stops believing the rest.
SAY"There are three things it does better, and I'll ask for those to be kept in the re-quote."
3
The pollution gap is on the policy YOU are recommending they keep.
"Environmental impairment liability — not included" is on the Santam schedule, all 27 vehicles. If you present this as a Petrosure failing you will be corrected, and it will cost you the room.
SAY"This one isn't about the quote. It's missing on what you have now, and I want to fix it whichever way you go."
4
The quote is a filling-station template.
Chilled stock, petrol pumps, underground tanks, till limits, petrol attendants, car wash, motor traders. They have no forecourt. Useful to you — but do not sneer at it in front of the client; it makes the underwriter look sloppy and the client wonder about your market.
DORaise it with Petrosure in writing. In the room, just say the schedule needs to describe a transport contractor.
5
You do not know their claims record.
"Loss History: Tba" on the quote. You have no loss ratio for this client — Santam's schedule does not carry one either. If they have had a bad two years, Petrosure's price moves and so might Santam's renewal. Ask the client directly.
ASK"What have you claimed in the last three years? I'd rather know now than have the underwriter tell me later."

The objections you'll hit — and the words that answer them
"Nine thousand a month is nine thousand a month."
It is. It's also seven percent. What you'd hand over for it is three quarters of the third party cover on every horse in the yard, and a hijack excess that goes from twenty thousand to three hundred thousand. One hijacked load costs you thirty-one months of that saving.
"Profile Risk have looked after us for years."
I'm sure they have. I'd just ask you to look at one line on your own schedule — environmental impairment liability, not included, on all twenty-seven vehicles. You move diesel. That's the cover that answers when a tanker goes over and it reaches a river.
"So you're telling me to do nothing?"
No — I've sent fifteen items back to the underwriter today and I want two things quoted that neither policy has. Doing nothing is what's been happening. What I'm not going to do is move you onto thinner cover to win your business.
"What if Petrosure won't budge?"
Then you stay where you are and we've still closed the pollution gap and looked at credit shortfall on the financed units. And I'll take your renewal to the market properly ahead of the next anniversary, with your claims record in hand instead of "TBA".
