The finding that matters most

Your liability cover stops at your front door

Your current public liability section is R5,000,000. Printed on the same page of your schedule are two restrictions. This is the first one, in full:

Old Mutual Insure · Public Liability section · Work away exclusion "The company will not indemnify the insured in respect of liability arising from any work carried out elsewhere than at the premises occupied by the insured."

Almost nothing your business does happens at your premises. The service is held at a church or a hall. The burial is at a municipal cemetery. The family is collected from home. The deceased is carried in your hearse, followed by your buses, along a public road, to a graveside your staff are working at.

⚠ What this means in practice A mourner injured at a graveside. A tent or a lowering device that fails at the cemetery. A member of the public hurt during a procession. On the wording you hold today, the public liability section does not answer for any of it, because none of it happens at premises you occupy.

Your schedule also carries a spread of fire exclusion — liability for a fire that spreads from your premises to a neighbour is excluded.
✅ What the market will do about it Bryte writes work away into the limit — its liability section reads "General and Tenants / Workaway R5,000,000" — for R295.83 a month.

Santam and Auto & General are silent on it, and we have asked both to confirm in writing whether liability away from the premises is covered before you decide. We would not place this policy anywhere without that answer.
Why we are raising it now rather than at renewal This is not a criticism of a quotation — it is a gap in the cover you have been paying for. It has been on your schedule all along and it costs very little to close. Moving insurer is the natural moment to fix it, which is why it is the first thing in this pack rather than a footnote.
One disclosure we are obliged to make Your public liability is written on a claims made basis with a retroactive date of 1 September 2025. That means it responds to claims first made against you now, for work going back to that date. A new policy would start its own retroactive date at inception, so approximately twelve months of past exposure would no longer be covered. Because your current retroactive date is recent, the loss is one year rather than many — but you should decide with that in front of you, and we will record your instruction in writing.

Missing everywhere — including on the policy you hold

What none of these covers

1 · Refrigeration failing, and what is inside it Deterioration of stock is not on your current policy and not on any of the three quotations. A funeral business refrigerates the deceased in its care. If a unit fails, or the power is off long enough, that is not a property claim and not a machinery claim — it is a deterioration claim, and there is no cover for it anywhere in front of you.
Worse: your current schedule carries an Electricity Grid Interruption exclusion which names "the deterioration of stock, food or other items" as excluded. Load-shedding and no deterioration cover is a live gap today, not a theoretical one. For this trade it is the characteristic loss, and its consequences are not only financial.
2 · Income, if a branch closes No business interruption cover on your current policy or on any quotation. Eleven trading premises and a programme costing R1.8 million a year, with nothing to replace the income if a branch burns and cannot trade for months. Salaries, rent and vehicle instalments continue regardless. This is a standing gap, not something a new insurer has removed.
Also worth closing
  • Machinery breakdown on the refrigeration plant itself — nowhere.
  • Money, fidelity (theft by an employee) and goods in transit not selected on Auto & General.
  • Employers' liability and group personal accident — with staff across eleven branches.
  • Theft: Santam covers one premises only; Bryte has no theft section at all.
  • An address on your schedule reads "Orlando, Cato Ridge 3680" — Cato Ridge is in KwaZulu-Natal. It has been copied onto the quotes. A misdescribed address can cost a claim.
Where Santam is ahead on detail
  • Undertaker's equipment R50,000, specifying two lowering devices by name.
  • Medical, catering and vacuum equipment separately itemised.
  • 63 vehicles against 61 on your current schedule.
Worth saying Santam and Bryte are the only schedules that read as though they were written for a funeral business rather than a generic commercial risk. Both name the lowering devices.
