01
The one section that decides this renewal

An e-hailing form on a scheduled bus service

Passenger liability is the cover that answers when a fare-paying passenger is injured or killed on one of your buses. For an operator carrying schoolchildren and commuters it is the most important line in the policy. We asked King Price to lift their limit to R2.5 million per event. Their answer, received 3 August, was that R2 million is their maximum.

But the limit was never the real question The wording on page 10 of their quotation has now been through three revisions unchanged. It reads:

• cover applies to "death of or bodily injury to e-hailing fare paying passenger"
• "The Passengers must be… in a vehicle which is active on an E-hailing platform"
• excluded: "any incident arising while the vehicle is… in use for a non-e-hailing platform"

What this means in practice If a scholar transport bus is involved in a serious accident, and the insurer applies this wording as written, the passenger liability section could decline the claim — not because the limit was too low, but because the vehicle was never on an e-hailing platform. A R2 million limit that does not respond is worth less than no limit at all, because it creates the belief that you are covered.
What we have asked for Written confirmation from the underwriter that this section responds to fare-paying passengers on scheduled commuter, WCED scholar and charter services, notwithstanding the e-hailing references — or alternatively that a standard passenger liability form be issued in its place. We have cited your own published operations as evidence of what the fleet actually does. Until that is in writing, we cannot advise you to move.
