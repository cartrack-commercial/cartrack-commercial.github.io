Our recommendation: King Price, with one correction King Price is the only one of the three whose policy is built for a school. Its business description reads "Schools / swimming schools", its liability limit is R25,000,000, and it writes teacher liability and pupil liability into the cover at the same limit. Bryte's liability section records your industry as "Unspecified". Page 03 sets out what that difference actually buys.
And the correction it needs, before anything is signed On every one of its 25 vehicle items King Price prints "Passenger liability: Private and LDV only". Your fleet is 21 Toyota Quantum Sesfikile 15 and 16 seaters, which are minibuses, not private cars and not light delivery vehicles. As written, the R3,000,000 shown against each bus may not respond. We have gone back to King Price to have this removed or confirmed in writing. We are not asking you to decide anything until it is resolved.
What is still outstanding We do not yet have your current Alpha schedule (ALPHACOM204975). So this pack tells you how the three quotes compare with each other and what each covers, but not yet whether any of them is an improvement on what you already have. That comparison follows as soon as the schedule reaches us.

The decisive cover · liability at school

What a school is actually sued for

For a school, and for a school for special-needs children in particular, liability is not slips and falls. It is duty of care: what happens between children, and between staff and children. Only one of these three quotes is written for that.

Why we are raising this rather than quietly pricing it A Toyota Quantum Sesfikile is a minibus. It is not a private car and it is not a light delivery vehicle. King Price has printed a passenger liability limit against every one of your buses and then restricted that cover to a class of vehicle you do not operate. The limit looks present on the schedule. On a plain reading it would not answer.

We have seen this exact pattern from this insurer once before, on another client running scholar and commuter transport. It is fixable, and it is the first thing we have asked them to fix.
Where that leaves the decision If King Price removes the restriction, it is the clear recommendation: the strongest schools liability in the market and passenger liability on the buses, for R1,542 a month more than Bryte. If it refuses, the choice becomes Bryte re-rated as a school, and we will bring you that conversation properly rather than quietly switching horses.

Three things to correct on the paperwork 1. The VAT number on the Bryte quote reads 9517532. That is seven digits. A South African VAT number is ten and begins with a 4.
2. About ten of the 21 buses carry no registration number on the Bryte schedule. Motor cover on a vehicle that cannot be identified is an argument waiting to happen at a claim.
3. Buildings differ by R4,667,734 between Bryte and Auto & General on the same premises. One of them is wrong, and if the policy is short, average applies and a claim pays proportionally.
Business interruption at R297,000 All three quote the identical figure, which usually means all three copied it from the current schedule rather than calculating it. For a school of this size it is worth checking what it is meant to represent before it is carried across a fourth time.
