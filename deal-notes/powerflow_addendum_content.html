How the three sections work together

Which one answers, and when

These are not three versions of the same thing. Each answers a different kind of claim, and the wordings are written to hand off to one another. That matters more than the limits, because it decides whether a claim is paid or argued.

SectionWhat it answersWhere it stops
Professional Indemnity
You designed or advised, and the design was wrong. The specification, the sizing, the advice on the system.It is not a workmanship cover. If the design was right and the installation was not, this section does not respond.
General Public Liability
Someone is injured, or property is damaged, while you are working. Includes work away from your premises.It does not pay for the consequences of the finished work. Once the job is handed over, this is not the section.
Defective Workmanship
The job is finished and handed over, and a defect in the work then causes damage or injury.It excludes anything arising from defective design, and it does not pay to redo the job itself.
The reason we recommend all three rather than one Read the two boundaries above together. Defective workmanship excludes design. Professional indemnity excludes workmanship. A real claim — an installation that fails and causes damage — rarely arrives neatly labelled as one or the other, and if the two sections sit with different insurers, or one of them is not bought at all, that argument becomes yours to fund. Held together on one policy, the handoff is internal. That is the single strongest reason to take the package rather than a section of it.
What defective workmanship does not do It pays for the damage the defect causes — not for rectifying or recalling the work itself, and not where the work simply did not produce the result expected. If a board you installed fails and burns a client's switchroom, the switchroom is the claim. Replacing the board is yours.
One operating condition worth passing to your site teams Cover for underground cables, pipes and services applies only where you have asked the relevant authorities and service providers about position and depth before work starts, and obtained the wayleaves. Where a service is not where the plans show it, the first amount payable is 10% of the claim with a minimum of R25,000. Where a service is not on the drawings at all, or was damaged through failure to take care around a known service, there is no cover. This is a site procedure, not a policy footnote.

Before you sign · four things, plainly

What this cover does not do

1 · It starts from day one. It does not look backwards. The quotation carries a retroactive date of inception and is written on a claims-made basis. Your Contractors All Risk policy says the same thing in its own words — "run-on cover: not included, only new projects as and when the policy becomes effective".
In plain terms: every installation you have already completed and handed over is not covered by this policy, for design or for workmanship. On solar and battery systems, defects tend to show up years after handover. We are asking the insurer to price a retroactive date. If they will not offer one, we want you to have this in writing before you decide — it is the most important sentence in this document.
2 · The liability section is largely cover you already have You carry R10,000,000 public liability on your Contractors All Risk policy, and further liability cover on the commercial policy. The insurer's rule is that defective workmanship can only be bought alongside their own public liability, so the R618.33 a month is effectively the price of admission to the defective workmanship section rather than new protection. We are asking whether they will write it over your existing liability instead. We would rather tell you that than sell it to you as an extra R10 million.
3 · We need your real annual turnover Your Contractors All Risk schedule declares an estimated annual turnover of R15,000,000 — yet a single noted contract on the same policy is R22,000,000. Professional indemnity is rated on turnover. If the declared figure is materially understated, the premium is wrong and, more seriously, the policy is open to challenge at the first claim. Please give us the actual figure before we place anything.
4 · Two small confirmations, and the deductibles The insurer has described your work as "design and advisory electrical installation works and solar" and asked us to confirm it. Given Linden Lane involves a 1001 kWh battery energy storage system, we want battery energy storage named on the policy in so many words, not left to be argued later.
Please also note the first amounts payable are higher than you are used to: R25,000 on a professional indemnity claim and 10% with a minimum of R25,000 on defective workmanship, against R5,000 on your contract works policy. On a R500,000 workmanship claim your share is R50,000.
Finally, the insurer requires confirmation that no claim or incident has arisen since 5 August 2026, when the proposal form was signed. If anything has, tell us now rather than later.
