The Naked Truth

DO NOT HAND
TO THE CLIENT

Six things that can turn this from a win into an argument. Number one can end the deal — raise it with Bryte before you raise the price with the client.

1
The sprinkler waiver. This is the deal-breaker.
The CIB fire section records "SURVEY DONE — Stacking Heights exceeds 3m. sprinkler system waived." Bryte is "subject to satisfactory survey". If their surveyor will not waive it on R73.3m of stock stacked over 3m, the client is being asked to sprinkler a warehouse.
DOSend Bryte the existing survey and waiver NOW and get their underwriter to confirm in writing before any figure goes out.
2
The quote states no motor excess whatsoever.
Not one figure. Current runs R4,000/R5,000 basic and 10% min R5,000 theft. A 33% motor saving on R404,885 more sum insured is exactly the shape of a quote with a heavier excess behind it.
DOGet the excess table in writing. If it is materially worse, the saving is not what it looks like and you need to know first.
3
Passenger liability halves on eight vehicles.
R5,000,000 → R2,500,000, passenger and unauthorised passenger. They also lose a stated R1,000,000 fire/explosion sub-limit — which may actually be a gain, since Bryte does not carve it out at all, but nobody has confirmed that.
SAY"One thing does come down and I would rather show you than have you find it. I have asked them to put it back and price it."
4
The liability section is rated on turnover R0 and wages R0.
Printed on the Bryte quote. Liability is rated on both, so R5,576 a year is a placeholder that will be re-rated after inception.
DOGet the real turnover and wage roll to Bryte now. A liability premium rated on a blank is not a premium.
5
Roadside is 33 times dearer than what they have.
R1,330/mo against R39.82/mo of motor assistance. Plus Office Buddy at R225 that nobody asked for. Together R15,960 a year — 3% of the programme.
SAY"Two lines in here are new and optional. Take them out and you save R123,000 a year instead of R106,000. Your call, not mine."
6
The footer says Hollard on a Bryte-titled quote.
Exactly the same discrepancy as the Hamisa Group quotes. Also: insured VAT and registration number are blank on the Bryte quote — both are on the CIB schedule (VAT 4760147696, reg 1995/001319/07).
DOConfirm in writing who carries the risk, and fill in the VAT and registration yourself before the client sees it.

The objections you'll hit — and the words that answer them
"Seventeen percent cheaper? What are they cutting?"
Fair question, and I checked it line by line. Goods in transit and machinery breakdown price to the same cent. Theft and business all risks are the same items at the same values. On the vehicles they are insuring R404,885 more than you carry today. One thing does come down — passenger liability — and I have asked them to put it back. The other thing I do not yet have is their motor excess table, and I am not giving you a final number until I do.
"We've been with CIB for years and never had a problem."
And you have a R30,000 claims record to show for it — that is exactly why this price is available. But you have also never had a claim from a site installation. Endorsement 001 on your liability section says the policy does not respond to work done away from your premises. That is most of what your technicians do.
"What's the catch on the survey?"
That is the honest risk and I will not pretend otherwise. Your current insurer surveyed and waived the sprinkler requirement even though stacking is over three metres. Bryte does its own survey. I have sent them your existing survey and waiver and I am asking them to confirm it in writing before you commit to anything.
"Can we just move the vehicles and leave the rest?"
You can, but it costs you more. The same eleven vehicles are R12,476 a year dearer bought on their own than inside the programme — two rates change on the Hino and the Crafter. And it would leave R73 million of stock and R75 million of annual carry where they are.
