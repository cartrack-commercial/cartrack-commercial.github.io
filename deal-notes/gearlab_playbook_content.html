The game plan

Play 01

This is not a comparison — there is no incumbent. So price is the least interesting thing you can say. Your value here is that you read two quotes and found that neither of them covers a customer's car on a hoist, in a business whose entire day is customers' cars on hoists.

Run the meeting in this order
1Ask them to describe a normal Tuesday. Car comes in, goes on the hoist, gearbox out. Get them saying it. Then show them the two lines that are switched off.
2Then the R50,000. Ask what the most expensive car in their workshop right now is worth, and how many are in at once. The number they give you is the number that should be on the policy.
3Then the entity question. Two quotes, two different registration numbers. You cannot bind either until you know which company trades.
4Then what's missing entirely — business interruption above all. R5.2m of stock at one address and no cover for the income if it burns.
5Price last, and don't recommend yet. ONE is R1,013 cheaper. Say so, and say you are not recommending on price until both markets have quoted the two missing extensions.
The question that sells the file
"What's on the hoist right now?"
Whatever they answer, it is worth more than R50,000 — and neither policy as issued pays for it. That single exchange is the whole meeting.
⚠ Before you present Tell both insurers in writing that this client has never been insured. ONE's approval makes inception conditional on a satisfactory claims history AND a previous policy schedule — neither of which can exist. An uninsured risk is rated differently. Get that confirmed now, not after they sign.

The Naked Truth

DO NOT HAND
TO THE CLIENT

Six things to know before you walk in. Number one means you should not be recommending either quote yet.

1
You cannot recommend either quote as it stands.
Both have the hoist/work-in-progress cover off. Recommending one on price and fixing the cover later is how a broker ends up on the wrong side of a claim.
SAY"I'm not going to tell you which one to take today. I'm going to tell you what's missing from both, get it priced, and then recommend."
2
The inception conditions are impossible to meet.
ONE wants a satisfactory claims history and a previous policy schedule. The client has never been insured, so neither exists. Underwriters reprice uninsured risks.
DOPut it to both insurers in writing this week and get the price confirmed on a no-prior-cover basis.
3
You do not know who the insured is.
Reg 2000/260824/07 on one quote, 2025/509941/07 on the other. And the Hollard VAT number is seven digits — it cannot be real.
ASK"Send me your CIPC registration certificate and your VAT certificate. I'm not binding a policy in a name I can't verify."
4
ONE is cheaper on theft by 43% for an identical R100,000.
R583.34 against R333.33. On the same sum insured that is a rating or a condition difference, not a bargain. There may be a warranty behind it.
DORead both theft sections' warranties and security requirements before you lean on the price gap.
5
ONE charges no Sasria on the motor traders section.
Hollard charges M4 Motor Ferries & Traders at R44.27. ONE charges F2 fire only. Sasria is statutory — an absent category usually means an undeclared exposure.
DOQuery it. R44 is nothing; a missing Sasria category at claims stage is not.
6
There is no business interruption on either quote.
R5,200,000 of stock, one premises, and a trade where the equipment is as hard to replace as the building. If it burns, the income stops and the salaries do not.
SAY"Nobody quoted this because nobody asked for it. I'd like to price it before you decide, so it's your choice and not an oversight."

The objections you'll hit — and the words that answer them
"Just give me the cheaper one, I've never had insurance anyway."
I can, and it's ONE at R2,718. But right now neither policy pays if a customer's car comes off your hoist, and both cap damage to customers' vehicles at fifty thousand. I'd rather spend a week getting that right than sell you something that doesn't answer when you need it.
"Fifty thousand sounds like plenty."
What's in your workshop right now? A gearbox rebuild on a modern SUV — that car's worth six, eight hundred thousand on its own, and you've got more than one in at a time. Fifty thousand doesn't replace a door on it. That limit needs to come from your yard, not from a form.
"Why do you need my registration documents?"
Because the two quotes name two different companies with two different registration numbers, and the VAT number on one of them isn't a real VAT number. If we insure the wrong entity, you'd find out at your first claim. Ten minutes of paperwork now.
"What else are you going to try and sell me?"
Nothing today. But you should know what isn't on either quote: if this place burns you've got no cover for the income while you rebuild, nothing on the hoists and presses themselves, and nothing on the diagnostic gear. I'll price them so you can decide — not so I can decide for you.
